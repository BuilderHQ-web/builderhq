## 1. Where we stand

The site today sells a vetted two-sided marketplace: homeowners upload plans, verified builders pay a one-off fee to unlock the address and documents, a maximum of three builders price the job, and the owner picks one. Its four loudest claims are all gatekeeping claims rather than capability claims: ABN and licence verification, the three-builder cap, privacy until unlock, and no commission. The Trust section says so outright, in the homeowner lens: `Marketplaces earn trust with structure, not slogans.` and `The marketplace sees a suburb and a scope, nothing more.` The gap is that the product now produces the thing the site assumes the customer arrives with. Scope of works is generated by the platform from the uploaded drawings, cited to document and page, reviewed by a human, and approved by the owner. Every builder then answers the same 93-question structured instrument and marks the owner's own scope line by line. Every tender is then scored on six published, weighted dimensions with a receipt ledger that reconciles to the score. None of that appears anywhere on the marketing surface. The site's own words for its output are `side by side`, `one format`, `a clean reference for both sides` and `Where Australia's residential builds get organised` — filing-cabinet language for what is now an assessment instrument. The precise gap: the site describes a filter; the product is a lifecycle.

**The hard availability caveat that governs everything below.** None of the three modules exists on `main`, which is the production and marketing branch. `src/modules/scope-engine/`, `src/modules/tenders/instrument.ts`, `src/modules/tenders/evaluation.ts` and `src/modules/scope/ontology.ts` are absent from `main`; `integration` is 89 commits ahead of it. The writer may plan the whole new narrative, but present-tense capability copy must not go live on production pages before the code does. Every dated or tensed sentence in the new copy needs a ship gate.

## 2. Product ground truth

Verified by executing the modules, not by reading comments. Counts marked ✅ were produced by running the code on 10 August 2026.

### Module 1 — Scope

**The vocabulary.** The BuilderHQ Scope Standard is version 1.2.0: **31 divisions, 256 items** ✅. Divisions run in build order — Preliminaries and site establishment, Approvals, Demolition, Alterations, Earthworks, Site services, Footings and ground floor structure, Retaining, Structural steel, Framing, Roofing, External walls and cladding, Windows, External doors, Insulation, Internal linings, Internal doors, Fix-out carpentry, Stairs, Joinery, Waterproofing, Tiling, Flooring, Painting, Plumbing and gas, Electrical and data, HVAC, Fire services, Appliances, External works, Landscaping and pool. Largest divisions: Electrical 15 items, Preliminaries 14, Approvals 12, Footings 12, External walls 12, Plumbing 12. Every item carries a `plain` field written for a homeowner, not a quantity surveyor. Verbatim from `preliminaries.site-establishment`: *"Setting up the site to work from: access, storage, and getting the block ready for trades."*

**The pipeline.** Three stages: classify (cheap model, first 8 pages of each document, returns document kind from nine types plus the AS 2870 soil classification and the NatHERS star rating where present), extract (page by page, records which Standard items each page evidences and what figures the page states), synthesise (folds everything onto the ontology with merged citations, gaps and conflicts).

**Two integrity rules, enforced in code as well as in prompt.** *Stated figures only* — the system records what the documents say, it never measures, scales or infers a quantity off a drawing. *No citation, no claim* — an evidenced item without at least one document-and-page citation is dropped in post-processing and logged.

**Citations are cross-examined.** A citation is `{documentName, page, revision}`, rendered as **"Structural S02, page 4, Rev B"**. It is then checked against the extract stage's own findings: a page that document never produced is a hard drop; a real page the extractor did not tag on that item keeps the citation but multiplies confidence by 0.7, which pushes it below the ops review floor so a human looks before the client does. An item that loses every citation is demoted out of the selection. Four further deterministic passes run alongside: conflict integrity, source authority (the drawn documents govern materials — a template wall type in an energy report cannot clad a building), note grounding, and a baseline date and client-name cross-examination. `SCOPE_CONFIDENCE_FLOOR = 0.65`.

**What a scope line is.** Three statuses: `evidenced`, `gap`, `not_expected`. Evidenced lines carry a depth grade: `full` (a builder can price it without assumption) or `partial` (shown or implied, but quantity, specification, performance or interfaces are incomplete), and a partial line carries a one-line `remaining` field saying exactly what is still needed. Verbatim from the synthesis prompt, and one of the best sentences available to the writer: *"Documented is not the same as priceable, and the difference is exactly what a builder needs to know."*

**Human gates.** Run lifecycle: `pending → classifying → extracting → synthesising → review → approved` (later `superseded`). Processing is resumable — a crash resumes from the last persisted document, a failed document does not fail the run. Ops verdicts per item are `confirmed` / `edited` / `removed`, and `approveRun` refuses while any item other than `not_expected` is still pending. Every verdict writes an append-only `scope_review_events` row.

**The readiness verdict.** `packReadiness()` returns **fixed price** or **budget only**, derived only from counts the run already produced, with no model call. Hard triggers: any unresolved high-severity conflict between documents, or a new build with no structural engineering in the register. Soft factors: more than 3 documents named by the pack but absent from it, or 40% or more of evidenced items graded `partial`. Budget-only fires on one hard trigger or two accumulated soft factors, and each factor prints as a plain sentence the client can read.

**The owner's side of it.** Before ops approval the owner sees only that reading is under way — never a half-reviewed pack. At approval, prior gap resolutions carry forward on a re-read (so only what changed asks again), and every gap that is the builders' ordinary work is auto-resolved to `builder_priced` before the client ever sees the pack. The owner then answers the remaining open questions with one of four resolutions: `allowance` (a locked sum every builder prices equally), `builder_priced`, `excluded`, `upload_later`. Completion gates on every gap resolved, no `upload_later` remaining, and a **6-question owner brief** answered ✅.

Which gaps the client is even asked about is a rule, not a judgement: a client-locked figure is allowed on prime-cost items always (they are selections) and on provisional-sum items only in landscaping, external works and joinery. Never on structure, earthworks, preliminaries or services, "where a client's guess would distort every quote." Separately, exactly six documents are treated as the client's to supply: soil/geotech, engineering design, acoustic report, development conditions, bushfire compliance, energy report.

**Addenda.** A revised pack on a live round diffs against the round's effective pack over the priceable set only — `added` / `removed` / `changed` — numbers itself sequentially per project (`Addendum 01`, `02`), flips the effective pack in the same transaction and marks the old run `superseded`, records a project event, and dispatches formal notice to every builder on the round. Output summary reads like **"2 lines added, 1 revised, 1 removed"**, or "No priceable lines moved".

### Module 2 — Tender submission

**`INSTRUMENT_VERSION = 3`** ✅. v1 was 10 sections / 53 questions, v2 was 11 / 99, **v3 is 11 sections / 93 questions**. All three remain in code so older tenders stay readable forever; question ids are permanent and never reused.

v3 composition ✅: 93 questions, 84 required, 17 gated on one earlier answer, 4 document-aware, 2 inline. Gating is single-level only by design — a question may depend on one earlier answer, never a chain. By type: 26 select, 15 bool, 12 items, 7 text, 7 confirm, 6 declare, 6 multi, 4 percent, 4 number, 2 currency, 1 month, 1 matrix, 1 amounts, 1 schedule.

What a builder actually faces, computed by running the module's own gate ✅:

| Round shape | In play | Asked regardless of any other answer | Required before any gate opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schedule round, all 3 reports on file | **86** | 71 | **63** |
| Schedule round, no reports on file | 84 | 69 | 61 |
| Legacy round, no approved schedule | 87 | 70 | 62 |

**The eleven modules, verified titles and in-play counts** (schedule round, all reports on file): Eligibility 9 · Project understanding 4 · Company credentials 13 · Commercial submission 17 · What's included 11 · What's not included 4 · Provisional sums and prime costs 4 · Programme 8 · Delivery 7 · Builder commentary 4 · Sign-off 5. The printed tender document renders **12 numbered modules** — these eleven plus "Additional documents", inserted before sign-off.

Design rule, verbatim from the module header and directly useful to the builder-facing copy: *"Neutral wording. The instrument never implies a 'right' answer — an honest 'excluded' must feel as acceptable as 'included'. Comparison does the judging, not the form."*

**The schedule — the mechanism that makes two tenders comparable.** On a round published through the scope gate, the builder does not self-declare a generic trade grid; they walk the client's own schedule line by line. Four states ✅, with the builder-facing labels: `documented` → **Included**, `allowance` → **Provisional sum**, `excluded` → **Excluded**, `na` → **N/A**. Per line the builder may attach an allowance figure (required and above zero when the state is `allowance`), an optional disclosed line price on an included line ("disclosure is a strength the builder chooses"), a one-line reason on N/A, and a free comment in any state that prints beside the mark on the document. Completeness gate: every tenderable line marked, every allowance carrying a figure above zero.

Schedule item kinds as the client's pack defines them: `evidenced`, `owner_allowance`, `owner_open`, `owner_excluded`. The platform derives per tender the split that matters most — **client allowance total vs builder allowance total** — because allowance money on the client's own lines is the round's exposure that every tender carries by instruction and never counts against a builder's firmness. It also tracks how many of the client's allowances each builder carried at the exact figure, repriced, priced firm, or excluded. The legacy grid, on rounds without an approved schedule, uses 27 trade rows ✅.

**Signature and declarations.** Module 11, all required: *"The information in this tender is true and complete"*, *"This offer stands and is capable of acceptance for its validity period"*, *"This tender was prepared without collusion with any other tenderer"*, plus signatory full name and role. Module 1 adds four affirmations under the same signature: authorised to submit for the company, builder licence current and covering work of this class, public liability insurance current, and — if the builder answered that they have not inspected the site — an explicit acknowledgement that the tender is priced without a full site inspection.

Every submitted tender gets a reference in the form **`BHQ-XXXXXXXX`** and a public verification page at `/verify/{tenderId}` that confirms the document is genuine — prepared through the BuilderHQ instrument by the named builder for the named project — **without revealing any of its content**.

### Module 3 — Evaluation

**Six dimensions, exact labels and weights** ✅. Use this wording verbatim.

| Label | Weight |
|---|---|
| Price firmness | 25 |
| Scope coverage | 25 |
| Preparation | 15 |
| Credentials & capacity | 15 |
| Delivery & aftercare | 12 |
| Programme confidence | 8 |

Each dimension scores 0–100 under a fixed rubric that totals exactly 100; the weights sum to exactly 100 and are "fixed, published, applied identically." Overall is `round(Σ(score × weight) / 100)`. The rationale is documented in code: cost blowouts and "I thought that was included" scope arguments are the two commonest dispute origins, so those two dimensions carry half the composite; programme carries least because "dates are the most renegotiated, least binding disclosure in any tender."

**Receipts.** Every dimension score carries a ledger of lines: `base` opens it, `delta`s adjust, an explicit `clamp` reconciles any floor or ceiling, `note`s carry context without points, and **`miss` lines show points that were available and not earned**. The invariant, asserted in dev: base + Σdeltas + Σclamps always equals the score.

**22 flags across 3 severities** ✅. Each carries a neutral `detail` (what the answer actually says) and most carry an `ask` (the question to put to the builder).
- 5 `high`: price is an estimate not fixed; escalation uncapped; exposure high (12% or more of the price can still move); deposit above the statutory cap; variations agreed verbally.
- 11 `attention`: exposure medium (5% or more); generic allowances; soil assumed; rock as a variation; permit fees; 3+ schedule lines marked N/A; no site inspection; partial documents; RFIs open; site lead carrying 6+ jobs; 3-month defects period.
- 6 `note`: escalation capped; capacity; crew tenure; loose aftercare; no weather allowance; short validity.

The deduped `ask` strings become the round's "Before you decide" agenda — the owner's pre-decision question list, generated from the flags rather than written.

**What the comparison surfaces.** Money picture: ex-GST and inc-GST price, exposure (PS + PC), client allowance vs builder exposure, `builderFirmPct` (100 minus the builder-originated share — the number that fairly compares tenders on a schedule round), escalation state (`none` / `capped` / `uncapped` / `undisclosed`), deposit vs statutory cap, validity days, overrun and variation margins. **Breakeven:** when the cheapest headline carries allowances a firmer rival priced in full, the engine computes the percentage those allowances must overrun before the paper saving is gone — pure division, no prediction. **The ladder:** for tenders in price order, what each step up actually buys, computed only from facts where the dearer tender is strictly better, capped at three gains per step, and it says so plainly if a step buys nothing measurable. **Scope disagreements:** trades or schedule lines where builders genuinely disagree on coverage. **Positions:** arithmetically-true superlatives, maximum two per tender, computed not written — *Lowest headline price*, *Fully priced*, *Firm beyond your allowances*, *Highest price certainty*, *Shortest build period*, *Strongest credentials*, *Broadest scope*, *Most prepared*, *Longest defects cover*. Plus `priceStory`, one computed sentence saying the thing this round most needs read.

**Two principles worth quoting in marketing, verbatim from the code.**
> *"An honest 'excluded' must never read worse than a vague inclusion. Disclosure is rewarded throughout: flagging a gap, itemising an allowance or declaring a weather add-on IMPROVES a builder's read, because it is the vague tender that wrecks projects, not the candid one."*

> *"Facts first, verdicts never... every number carries RECEIPTS — the disclosed facts it was computed from — so an architect can defend any line of it to their client, and a builder could read their own evaluation without feeling ambushed."*

**What the owner receives** — seven navigable sections ✅, with the shipped headings and kickers: *About the builders* / "Checked before you compare"; *The overview* / "What this round comes down to"; the per-tender cards / "Where the money stands"; *The decision grid* / "Side by side", whose own description is *"Every row is the builders' own answers, lined up."*; *Where the tenders differ* / "The same project, read differently"; *What the builders brought* / "Beyond the price"; *Before you decide* / "The pre-decision agenda".

### Verification (what is actually wired)

- **ABN → Australian Business Register**, national, via a Cloudflare Worker proxy. Returns entity name, ABN status, GST registration, state, postcode. 90-day TTL.
- **Builder licence → Victorian Building Authority only.** Returns accreditation ID, status, limitation (e.g. "Domestic Builder - Unlimited"), commencement and expiry. **There is no NSW, QLD, ACT, WA, SA, TAS or NT licence register integration.**
- Locks compute at read time and invalidate the moment the builder edits the input. Names are matched with normalisation (entity suffixes stripped), not strict equality.
- **189 unit tests pass** across instrument, evaluation, schedule, scope advice and scope-engine analysis ✅ (run 10 August 2026).

### The proof points a copywriter may safely use

1. **256 scope lines across 31 divisions**, versioned (Scope Standard v1.2.0), every line carrying a plain-English sentence written for a homeowner.
2. **"No citation, no claim."** An item is dropped unless it can point to a document and a page — then the citation is cross-examined against the extraction stage's own page findings. A page that was never read is a hard drop; a real page the extractor did not tag takes a 0.7 confidence penalty that pushes it below the human-review floor. This discipline is unusual and it is enforced in code, not just in a prompt.
3. **"Stated figures only — it never measures off a drawing."**
4. **A human signs off every line before a client sees it.** Approval is refused while a single non-trivial item is unreviewed, and every verdict writes an append-only audit row.
5. **"Documented is not the same as priceable."** Every evidenced line is graded full or partial; a partial line says in one sentence what is still missing; the pack returns fixed-price or budget-only with plain-English reasons. A new build with no structural engineering is automatically budget-only.
6. **Six questions for the owner. Sixty-three for the builder.** Everything that is the builders' ordinary work is auto-resolved before the client sees the pack; the client is asked only what a client can sensibly answer.
7. **93 structured questions across 11 modules, and every tender on the round answers the same ones.** 86 in play on a typical round, 63 required before a single conditional question opens.
8. **Included / Provisional sum / Excluded / N/A, line by line, on the client's own schedule** — not a trade grid the builder invents.
9. **Six weighted dimensions, published, applied identically.** No secret sauce.
10. **Every score shows its working**, including the points a builder did not earn and why.
11. **Disclosure is rewarded, not punished.**
12. **The superlatives are computed, not written** — attached only when arithmetically true within that round.
13. **The breakeven test** — how far the cheapest tender's allowances must overrun before the saving disappears.
14. **Scope never changes quietly** — numbered addendum, diffed priceable set, old pack superseded in the same transaction, every builder formally notified.
15. **Every tender is verifiable** — a `BHQ-` reference and a public page confirming the document is genuine without revealing a word of its contents.
16. **The unlock prices are real and per-project-type:** renovation $49, extension $99, single dwelling $149, multi dwelling $199. The site's existing "from $49" is the renovation floor and is accurate.

### Claims to avoid

**Availability.** Do not describe any of the three modules in the present tense as something a visitor can use today until the code is on `main`.

**Accuracy and performance.** Never quote an accuracy, recall or precision figure for the document reading — the golden-set format and scorer exist but `eval/scope-golden/packages/` contains only `_example`, so **no accuracy claim is currently supportable**. No speed claims ("scope of works in 24 hours", "reads a pack in minutes") — nothing in the code establishes a wall-clock time. No volume or outcome claims ("X projects tendered", "owners save Y%") — no production data was read. Do not say a builder "finishes in about thirty minutes"; that is an author's design note in a code comment, not a measurement.

**What the reading does and does not do.** Do not say it "reads every page of every document" without qualification — extraction works within an 800-extraction-page and 30-document budget per run, and classification reads only the first 8 pages of each document. (The product's own PDF footer already says "reads every page of the client's documents"; that is existing product copy and marketing should not amplify it into a headline.) Do not claim it takes off quantities, measures, scales, or produces a bill of quantities with numbers — the printed schedule *reads like* a bill of quantities but is not a take-off. Do not claim it produces cost estimates or a price; it produces scope, builders produce prices. Do not claim it detects every gap — the synthesis prompt explicitly instructs the model not to attempt an exhaustive sweep at that stage.

**Verification.** **Do not say "we verify every builder's licence."** Licence verification is Victoria only. ABN verification is national; licence verification is not. Do not present insurance answers as verified — public liability, home warranty eligibility and workers compensation are **declared under signature, not register-checked**, and the code labels them accordingly.

**Statutory.** The only statutory check in the evaluation is a deposit cap for three states: **VIC 5%, NSW 10%, QLD 5%**. There is no ACT, WA, SA, TAS or NT cap, so no deposit flag fires at all in those jurisdictions — significant given the Canberra builder network. The flag copy says "for a contract of this size" but the code applies a flat per-state percentage with no size threshold; that is arguably a copy bug to fix in-product, and marketing must not repeat it. Do not describe the platform as giving legal, contractual or financial advice.

**Positioning.** Do not say the platform "picks the best builder" or "recommends a winner" — it ranks, scores and flags, and the surface is literally titled "Before you decide". Do not say builders are "scored against each other"; each dimension is scored against a fixed rubric independently, and only the superlatives and the ladder are relative. Do not imply the AI approves the scope — ops approves it, then the owner accepts it, two human gates.

**Unverified, carried through as flags.** No accuracy number exists for the extraction pipeline. The model ids in the pipeline were read from source, not called. No real-world run cost, duration or document volume was read. Only 5 pure unit files were run (189 tests); the integration suites need a dev database and were not run. Code comments referring to "twelve modules" and "seventy questions" were never updated — the verified figures are 11 question sections, 12 printed modules, and 63 required / 86 in play.

### Colour

One theme layer, light-first, plus one scoped override. `--color-bg` is `#f4f1ea`, a warm greige. Surfaces: `--color-surface-1` `#fbf9f4` ("warm paper", deliberately not white), `-2` `#f6f3ec`, `-3` `#efeae0`. Borders are ink-alpha hairlines, never grey hex: `rgba(24,34,44,0.10)` subtle, `0.14` base, `0.22` strong. Text: `--color-text` `#161c22` (15.2:1 on cream), `--color-text-muted` `#48535d` (6.97:1), `--color-text-subtle`, `--color-text-faint`, and `--color-text-dim` `#78828d` (**3.46:1 — fails AA for body copy**).

`.lp-light` is a class wrapper on marketing trees (the landing, `page-shell.tsx`, `/verify`, `/build-brief`, `/partners/welcome`, `/partners/preview`) that re-resolves 15 neutral tokens so every `bg-surface-1` / `text-text-muted` utility flips automatically. The meaningful changes: `--color-surface-1` becomes pure `#ffffff`, the modal scrim flips from dark to cream, subtle borders go one notch finer, muted text lightens to `#53606b`. **Keep it — it is the cheapest theming lever in the codebase.**

Teal as type is `#0a7d73` (4.44:1 on cream, 5.01:1 on white); teal as fill is `#00d4c8` always paired with `--color-accent-contrast` `#031118` (10.25:1). ### Motion

Easings: `--ease-out` `cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1)` (brand, ×13), `--ease-out-soft` `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` (Reveal, RoleSwap, hero), `--ease-in-out-soft`, and `--ease-spring` which is defined and unused on marketing.

Durations: 140–180ms hovers (`160ms` and `180ms` are the house values), 200–320ms chevrons and card lifts, 420ms nav shadow, `350ms in / 180ms out` for the copy re-tune, 500–700ms testimonial slide and scroll reveals, 900ms canvas glow crossfade and section divider, 1.0–1.15s hero entrance and the wash, 4.6–5.2s ambient loops.

`Reveal` is the shared scroll entrance: opacity 0→1, y 16→0, 700ms, `viewport={{ once: true, amount: 0.15 }}`, documented as "slow and soft… never bouncy or aggressive". `RoleSwap` is the re-tune primitive: `AnimatePresence mode="wait"` keyed on role, old copy exits 8px up over 180ms, new copy rises 10px in over 350ms, `stagger` cascades children at 60ms, `initial={false}` so first paint never animates, and it is explicitly documented as "furniture, not fireworks — no scale, no blur". **The single most reusable piece in the folder.**

Ambient devices: a fixed `Canvas` under everything — base `#f4f1ea`, four stacked role-glow layers (three radial gradients each) crossfading over 900ms, a 64px blueprint grid at 0.5 opacity under a radial mask, and an inline SVG `feTurbulence` grain at 0.035. `RoleWash`, a one-shot re-light on lens change: a radial flood plus a 112° diagonal sweep over ~1.15s. `SectionField` in three variants (`warm`, `dots`, `teal`), each bracketed top and bottom by an `rgba(24,34,44,0.07)` hairline so band edges read as a deliberate seam. `SectionDivider`, a hairline that scale-X draws itself in from both edges over 900ms carrying a mono numeral and an 11px letterspaced label. `ScrollProgress`, a 2px spring-damped bar above the nav in an accent-to-accentSoft gradient.

**Reduced motion is handled thoroughly, in four layers:** a global `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` forcing `0.001ms !important`; `scroll-behavior: auto` under the same query; `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">` wrapping the entire landing; and an explicit `matchMedia` check on the role-change scroll-to-top. **Lenis smooth scroll was removed** with a written rationale in `layout.tsx`: "it fought the role-morph choreography and JS scroll-jacking reads 'techy' to the exact audience the page needs to reassure." The `.lenis*` CSS rules remain as dead code.

### The honest read: what looks institutional, what looks like a startup

**Institutional.** The colour discipline is genuinely mature — a warm greige canvas with 15:1 near-black ink and ink-alpha hairlines is the palette of a professional-services firm, and nothing is saturated except the CTA. The documented teal rule is exactly the kind of constraint that separates a design system from a colour picker, and v2 enforces it. Hairline-and-seam construction reads like a printed prospectus. **The numbered chapter dividers `01–08` in mono on a fading rule are the single strongest institutional signal on the page: they say *document*, not *funnel*.** Mono is used sparingly and correctly. Instrument Serif on the Build Brief reads like a publication with an archive. Motion knows when to stop, and the reduced-motion diligence exceeds most Series-B companies. Measure control in `ch` shows someone thinking about reading rather than filling a viewport.

**Startup.** The role morph is the biggest liability: a page whose ambient lighting, headline hue, stat colours, divider tint and scroll bar all recolour when you say who you are is a product demo of the website itself, and `RoleWash` is a lens flare. Four brand hues is four brands; a council, a bank or realestate.com.au has one. Type is undersized — roughly a third of all sized text is under 13px, where established organisations set 16–18px body because their readers are 45–65 and the content is consequential. Simultaneously the headlines are too big: a `5.4rem` hero at `-0.045em` over a `14.5px` mobile sub is a 6:1 range between the loudest and quietest voice, and tight negative tracking is specifically the Linear/Vercel/Framer signature. One font does everything, and that font is Geist — literally Vercel's typeface, the house font of the startup category — while Instrument Serif is loaded, paid for, and used on one component. Eight consecutive full-viewport sections plus a sticky card deck is presentation-deck choreography: the page cannot be skimmed and density is impossible, while institutions publish dense pages because they have things to say. The glowing cyan CTA with an inner highlight is a neon-on-dark button that survived the flip to light and is the loudest object in an otherwise very quiet composition. Sixteen bespoke shadows against two named utilities, `#f4f1ea` hard-coded four times, an unused container token and a parallel grey ramp in `spine.tsx` mean a designer cannot rely on token names alone.

**Two contrast failures at token level.** `--color-text-dim` `#78828d` is 3.46:1 on cream and carries real copy: section-divider labels, footer column heads, the Trust footnote. The homeowner and architect `accent` hues (3.06:1 and 3.05:1) carry eyebrow text. For an organisation positioning on trust and process this is table stakes, and it is a cheap fix — `accentSoft` passes in all four lenses.

**The reader's bottom line:** the quiet layer of this system is institutional-grade and better than most; the loud layer is unambiguously startup. The two highest-leverage moves toward "established" are to raise body type to 16–17px while cutting the display maximum to about 3.5rem, and to make the role lens change *copy* without changing the *room's lighting*.

## 7. Constraints and gotchas

**Brand voice, already in force.** No em dashes. No exclamation marks. No hype words. Curly apostrophes. Short sentences. Plain Australian English. Serious, mature, institutional — council or realestate.com.au grade. Two live violations to fix while rewriting: an em dash at `architect-tender/page.tsx:130` and another at `book-a-call/page.tsx:97`. The FAQ also carries an en dash in `Most owners publish in 2–5 minutes.` — worth a consistency ruling.

**Legal and compliance.**
- **All twelve testimonials are fabricated** and both `content.ts` and `testimonials.tsx` carry an explicit warning that they breach the Australian Consumer Law's misleading-conduct provisions. They must be replaced or removed, not merely rewritten with new invented content.
- `/start`'s layout metadata claims BuilderHQ is `the only marketplace where verified Australian builders tender for your residential project` — an absolute superiority claim that must be substantiated or softened.
- The `/start` ProofBand numbers `50+ Active builders` and `$50M+ Tenders uploaded` are hardcoded and unverified.
- `/terms` §3 says "marketplace only" while the product ships computed scores. New marketing claims must not outrun the terms; flag anything the writer adds that §3, §5, §6 or §7 does not cover.

**Routing and mechanics that must not break.**
- Sentinel hrefs `#join-architect`, `#join-finance` and `#request-intro` are intercepted by `partner-form.tsx` to open the partner modal. CTA labels can change freely; the hrefs cannot.
- The `#nav-role-dock` element id is a load-bearing measurement contract with the flying label.
- Every Spine card CTA reuses the hero primary CTA label — change one, change five.
- `authedHref` overrides the primary CTA in Hero, Spine and Close with `Open your dashboard` for signed-in visitors. Any new CTA in those three sections needs the same auth-aware behaviour.
- `/for/[audience]` renders the *same* landing composition, so any home-page change propagates to three campaign routes with different metadata.
- The Network builder CTA is a `switchTo` button that changes the page's lens rather than a link; it dies with the role selector.
- `/estimate_request_landing_page` has its URL preserved verbatim from Bubble so a live Google Ads destination keeps working. The file comment warns against renaming. `/guide` and `/start` are also live paid destinations.
- `SectionTracker` fires `section_viewed` against a hard-coded id list (`hero, choose, how, trust, network, ecosystem, faq`) that is already out of sync — Problem is `#story`, and Proof, Testimonials, Build Brief and Close have no ids. Re-derive it from the new section set.
- Analytics events already in place: `lens_selected` with a source, `section_viewed`, `close_cta`.

**SEO layer that must move with the copy.**
- `src/lib/seo.ts` emits Organization and WebSite JSON-LD on every marketing page. Its `SITE_DESCRIPTION` is the canonical machine-readable description of the company and currently reads: `BuilderHQ is Australia's residential construction tendering platform. Homeowners and developers list their project once, with full architectural plans, and up to three verified builders price it on the same scope, so quotes are comparable like for like.` Its `slogan` is `Upload once. Compare like for like. Build.` Its `knowsAbout` list is residential construction tendering, comparing builder quotes, home building in Australia, renovations and extensions, verified residential builders, construction finance. **This is the layer generative engines quote, and none of it mentions architect-run rounds or the lifecycle.**
- `public/llms.txt` is hand-maintained and repeats the stale framing, including `[For building designers](https://builderhq.com.au/for/architects): the Preferred Design Partner Network, featured and referred at no cost.`

- **Indexable but missing from `sitemap.ts`:** `/guide`, `/owneradvisory` (explicitly `index: true`), `/estimate_request_landing_page`, `/book-a-call/confirmed`, `/start`, `/delete_account`, `/build-brief/feed.xml`.

None of this is placeholder and none of it should be replaced with invented partners.

1. **Three different legal entities appear in production copy.** `/about`, the v2 footer and `/partners/preview` say `BuilderHQ · ABN 70 697 584 722`. `/terms` §18, `/privacy` §1 and §16 say `BuilderHQ Technologies Pty Ltd · ABN 33 612 815 139`. 2. **Two names for one thing.** `/guide` is titled `The Melbourne Build Brief` while `/build-brief` is the live weekly publication called The Build Brief.
3. **Four design systems in the marketing surface.** MarketingPageShell (cream, Geist, v2 chrome); the Fraunces + Inter ad-lander family (`/guide`, `/estimate_request_landing_page`, `/book-a-call`); the bespoke dark `/owneradvisory` and `/architect-tender`; and `/delete_account` still on the deprecated v1 dark chrome. Defensible for paid traffic, but there is no shared "this is BuilderHQ" moment across them.
4. **The ad landers are cul-de-sacs.** `/guide`, `/estimate_request_landing_page`, `/owneradvisory` and `/architect-tender` all link home only via an absolute `https://builderhq.com.au`. There is no path from a downloaded guide or a delivered estimate into the platform.

**Positioning constraints from the founder, restated because the writer must hold them through every line.** Architect framing must be careful: not every architect runs a formal tender process, and nothing may imply anything negative about architects referring builders or about referral commissions. Builder framing must be careful: the "bad builders can't undercut you" benefit has to be expressed without sounding bitter or disparaging — the evaluation engine's own principle gives the writer the honest version of this, that disclosure is rewarded and a vague inclusion is what wrecks projects, not a candid exclusion. And BuilderHQ is no longer positioned as a marketplace, even though the marketplace still exists inside it.

## 8. Open decisions for the strategist

These are the genuine forks. Each needs a judgement call before the rewrite can start.

**1. Does the four-lens role morph survive?** It is roughly a third of the page's complexity — a full viewport of scroll, the flight choreography across five files, the dock measurement contract, the scroll-to-top coupling, the four-way content fork, and four brand hues. The design reader's verdict is that it is the page's single biggest "startup" signal. But it is also the only mechanism that lets one page speak to four genuinely different buyers. The middle path exists: keep the nav dropdown and the copy fork, delete the selector section, the flight and the room-relighting.

**2. Which audience leads?** The site is built homeowner-first. The product's strongest new capabilities — a cited scope, a defensible evaluation with receipts — are worth most to an architect or building designer running a round for a client, and the strategy stated in the founder's brief treats them as a demand channel. Leading with the professional changes the hero, the ordering of everything, and which mockup goes in the hero frame.

**3. What is BuilderHQ's one-sentence category, now that "marketplace" is retired?** The current candidates already in the codebase are `the trusted layer for residential construction`, `tender infrastructure`, and `where projects start`. This decision cascades into `SITE_DESCRIPTION`, `slogan`, `llms.txt`, the Ecosystem section, the Trust intro and `/terms` §3.

**4. Three modules or four steps?** The Spine's stacked deck is the strongest structural asset on the page and is built for four cards. The story is three modules. Either add a fourth beat (award, or the marketplace itself, or addenda), collapse the deck to three, or map the modules onto a different section entirely. Note the "Four steps." heading is hard-coded while the counter derives from the array length.

**5. Where does the marketplace go?** It still exists, it still funds the business at $49–$199 per unlock, and it is what most builders come for. But the positioning says BuilderHQ is not a marketplace. Options: a peer capability alongside the three modules; a distribution layer underneath them; a builder-only story that never appears in owner or architect copy; or a separate page.

**6. Do the existing testimonials stay?** The founder's brief says self-generated testimonials may stay and can be rewritten. The code carries an explicit Australian Consumer Law warning saying the current twelve are fabricated and must be replaced with real attributable quotes before deploy. These two positions have to be reconciled. The stepped-staircase stat layout is worth salvaging even if the quotes go, and the alternative slots are a case study, a computed metrics band, or the partner logo wall.

**7. Does `/architect-tender` come out from behind the noindex, or does a public sibling get built?** It is the closest page on the site to the current product and it is currently invitation-gated and uncrawlable. If architect signup is genuinely open, the `By invitation` chip, the noindex, and the "we run tender for you" framing all have to change — and its 10-council marquee, its planning-permit angle and its "Tender, structured." tagline are assets worth keeping either way.

**8. How much of the Preferred Partner programme stays in the main narrative?** The register at `/partners` is honest, live and useful, and the finance lens exists only to serve it. But it is a referral-marketing story sitting inside a tender-infrastructure page, and the architect lens currently promises tendering in the hero then spends four Spine cards explaining a promotion programme. Either the two stories get explicitly related in one sentence, or the partner programme moves out of the home page entirely.

**9. Does the Build Brief strip stay on the home page?** It is the best-designed block on the site and the highest-value trust artefact, and it is all-or-nothing — the broadsheet metaphor is the credibility argument, so it cannot be re-skinned as something else. The question is whether a weekly publication interstitial belongs in the middle of a product narrative, or whether it earns a place only in the nav and footer.

**10. How far toward "established" does the type system move?** The two named levers are raising body type from 14.5px to 16–17px and cutting the display maximum from 5.4rem to about 3.5rem, plus putting Instrument Serif to work on display where it is already loaded and paid for. Both are cheap. Both change the page's entire character, and the serif decision in particular is a brand decision rather than a typographic one.

**11. What is the ship gate?** Nothing described in section 2 exists on `main`. The writer needs to know whether they are writing the page that goes live the day the modules ship, or a page that goes live sooner and describes the current production capability honestly. That determines every verb tense in the document.

**12. Which legal entity is BuilderHQ?** Three appear in shipped copy. Somebody has to say which, and then it propagates to `/about`, the footer, `/terms`, `/privacy`, `/partners/preview` and six ad-lander footers.
